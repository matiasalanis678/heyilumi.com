How it works

From the first hello to the hundredth day.

Ilumi is a tutor for reading, writing and math, for ages 4 to 8. Here is what you do, what your child does, and what Ilumi does in between.

Inside a session

The same six steps, every time.

Your child always knows what comes next. What changes is the story. Today’s: a fox, a hen, and only one box.

  1. Two or three things from days or weeks ago, picked from your child’s own history, so nothing they learned fades.

  2. One new idea, shown and said aloud by the guide. Only one, so there is room to practise it.

  3. The story moves only while your child reads it aloud, a word at a time. On math days, they slide in exactly the right number of acorns.

  4. The guide asks one question about the story, and listens. It isn’t a quiz, and the answer is never scored.

  5. The swallow asks for words from the story they just read. A word is easiest to spell right after reading it.

  6. Camp turns to night. If your child mastered something, their rank moves. If not, tomorrow picks up where today left off.

The rhythm

One session a day, on purpose.

When today’s session is done, camp turns to night and the rabbit naps by the fire. No bonus round, and nothing to collect for coming back.

  • Reading and math take turns

    Whichever day you come back, it’s the other one’s turn. Writing rides along on reading days.

  • Ilumi picks the subject

    Your child never has to choose, so the subject they like less still gets its turn.

  • Want more? Do it together

    Your report suggests one thing to try away from the screen. “I spy something that starts with /m/.”

Moving up

Three good days, then the next step.

A step counts as learned when your child has shown it in three sessions, on three different days. Not one lucky afternoon.

  • Old steps come back

    What they’ve learned returns in the warm-up: soon after, then days later, then weeks. If one has slipped, it goes back into practice.

  • Never for showing up

    Their rank grows only when they master something. Days in a row, and time spent, earn nothing.

When it’s hard

Nobody marks it wrong. The guide helps.

No red X, no buzzer, no score. When something doesn’t come, the guide shows the right way, and your child tries again.

  • Reading, with the rabbit

    1. First“That one’s a tricky word. Take your time, I’m still here. Try sounding it out.”
    2. Then“Let’s read it together: pup.”

    The story carries on. The word is kept as one they had help with, not one they read.

  • Math, with the squirrel

    1. First“Let’s count them in together: one, two, three, four, five. Your turn.”
    2. Then“Here’s another one. Take your time.”

    The next one is a little smaller, with the counters showing. The answer is always theirs to make, never picked from a list.

  • Writing, with the swallow

    1. First“You got /f/ and /sh/.”
    2. Then“In ‘fish’, that sound is written I. Watch me.”

    It names what they got right before it shows what was missing. Spelling by sound is a step on the way, not a mistake.

And when the same mix-up keeps coming back

Once is noise. If your child keeps reading “ship” as “sip”, on more than one day, Ilumi notices, and the next words are pairs that differ by just that sound. They are never told what they keep missing. They just get the practice for it.

  • shipsip
  • shopsop
  • shellsell
